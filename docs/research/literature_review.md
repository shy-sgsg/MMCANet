## Flow posterior transport: distinguish exact conditioning from guided trajectories — 2026-10-29

Kim et al.'s [FlowDPS](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Kim_FlowDPS__Flow-Driven_Posterior_Sampling_for_Inverse_Problems_ICCV_2025_paper.html) is a flow-prior posterior-guidance method: it uses a flow Tweedie decomposition, applies a measurement gradient to the denoised estimate, and mixes stochastic noise into the noise estimate. Its evaluated operators are linear Gaussian image-inverse problems on natural-image data; the method does not test SAR speckle or optical guidance.

Xu et al.'s 2026 [posterior-transport preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24516) derives the exact source-reweighting identity for a deterministic diffeomorphic flow prior and analyzes approximate trajectory corrections. In its controlled 2D Gaussian-mixture example, its FlowDPS-style likelihood-guidance comparison misweights posterior modes, while exact source reweighting reaches the Monte Carlo floor. The same manuscript says source importance sampling becomes intractable at high dimension and explicitly does not certify its proposed image-scale heuristic as a posterior sampler. This is a toy-theory result, not evidence about SAR or all guidance solvers.

The relevant methodological gap is therefore not “add a likelihood gradient and call the output a posterior sample.” A future, approved posterior route would need a valid task-specific likelihood and predeclared distributional/uncertainty diagnostics. The reviewed Gaussian linear inverse models do not validate the local normalized-PNG pseudo-target model, optical-conditioned SAR priors, or mismatch-safe restoration. See the [full source audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/flow_posterior_transport_source_review_20261029_v1/report.md) and D-2026-10-29-013.

## Generative-method roles relevant to the proposed SAR-output question — 2026-10-29

Primary-source review distinguishes Conditional Flow Matching as a training objective, Rectified Flow as a choice of transport path, and DPS/FlowDPS as posterior-guidance procedures around a learned prior ([Flow Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747), [Rectified Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003), [DPS](https://openreview.net/forum?id=OnD9zGAGT0k), [FlowDPS](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Kim_FlowDPS__Flow-Driven_Posterior_Sampling_for_Inverse_Problems_ICCV_2025_paper.html)). These general image/inverse-problem methods do not validate a local SAR observation likelihood, optical-conditioned SAR prior, or scatterer-safe fallback. Adaptive diffusion guidance has general posterior-sampling precedent ([AISTATS 2026 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/azangulov26a.html)), but not an optical reliability signal for SAR restoration. Keep these as method candidates behind the local optical-utility and data/target gates; see the [supporting audit](RESEARCH_CHARTER_SUPPORTING_AUDIT_20261029.md).

## γ-Bridge: look-parametric Gamma diffusion is SAR-only despeckling prior art — 2026-10-29

Hu et al.'s arXiv preprint proposes a look-indexed Gamma–Lévy diffusion bridge with exact multiplicative-Gamma marginals under its assumed intensity observation model. Training uses natural-image crops with synthetic single-look Gamma corruption and no real SAR images; the paper reports zero-shot transfer on 33 real-SAR tiles from six sensors using released PNG/JPG products as intensities and no-reference metrics. This is direct prior art for Gamma-structured generative SAR despeckling, but it has no optical input or spatial super-resolution and does not validate a calibrated likelihood for the local normalized-PNG data. The paper links an author code repository, but that URL returned 404 during this review. Treat all performance claims as author-reported. See the [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22719) and [D-2026-10-29-008](DECISION_LOG.md#d-2026-10-29-008--add-gamma-bridge-as-sar-only-gamma-diffusion-prior-art).

## MD-Glow: SAR multi-task despeckling and SR (indexed full-text screen; 2026-10-25)

Takao's MD-Glow is direct SAR-only multi-task prior art: a shared invertible Glow encoder/decoder feeds task-specific feature-translation modules for despeckling and ×2 SR. It synthesizes single-look multiplicative Gamma-speckled training samples from SEN1-2 optical imagery, uses bicubic ×2 inputs for SR and random downsampling for the despeckling task, and trains with an L1 objective. The paper states 5,025 256×256 training images and 525 randomly selected SEN1-2 test images, but the reviewed text does not establish source-scene-disjoint membership. Its quantitative tables evaluate despeckling, not a joint-SR output: MD-Glow is best on the stated LR set (25.412 dB / 0.623 SSIM) and second on the original-resolution set (25.726 / 0.654, below SAR-CAM's 26.012 / 0.683). The multi-task ablation is scale/metric dependent; the real-data assessment uses ENL in four regions across two images without a clean reference. The figure that chains despeckling into SR is qualitative and has no SR metric table. ### External multitemporal SAR reference candidate (2026)

Díaz-Paz et al.'s 2026 *Data in Brief* dataset pairs 512×512 Sentinel-1 IW GRD-HD VV/VH single-acquisition inputs from both orbit directions with same-location multitemporal averages using 5, 10, 15, 20, or 25 acquisitions. The article does not identify which acquisition indices contribute to each reference stack, however, so this schema does not establish input exclusion or target independence. The Mendeley record states CC BY 4.0 and lists ESA WorldCover alongside SAR inputs and references. The paper says samples are globally selected and rejects candidate regions whose mean VH backscatter is below −20 dB. It describes a Python/Google Earth Engine workflow, but no code repository is linked in the accessible article. This is a candidate for a SAR-only despeckling/reference feasibility track, not an optical-guided or super-resolution dataset. Several protocol details prevent adopting it as a reference without further audit. Its filename and metadata schema suggests a practical route to audit scene-area groups and acquisition timing once the release files are available, but the paper does not publish a per-`GT_x` list of contributing acquisition indices. It specifies the processing order as rescaling the SAR images to 0–255, registering them to a designated reference acquisition, and then performing multitemporal fusion. It does not identify the mapping into that range, the exact representation/domain used for averaging, the registration algorithm or residual error, or per-target acquisition membership. The paper identifies Google Earth Engine and the ESA Sentinel-1 Copernicus collection but does not name the Earth Engine collection ID. **Conditional shared-noise derivation (not a measurement of either release):** Let a static reflectivity value be $\rho$, and let each same-domain intensity observation be $I_i=\rho S_i$, where the $S_i$ are independent, unit-mean multiplicative speckle terms with variance $1/L$. For an $N$-acquisition mean $T_N=N^{-1}\sum_i I_i$, if the paired noisy input $I_1$ is included, then $\operatorname{Cov}(I_1-\rho,T_N-\rho\mid\rho)=\rho^2/(NL)$ and the conditional correlation between those two noise residuals is $1/\sqrt{N}$. If the target stack excludes the input and acquisitions are independent, that covariance is zero under this model. For $N=5,10,15,20,25$, the inclusion-case correlations are approximately 0.447, 0.316, 0.258, 0.224, and 0.200. The exact MSE to a pseudo-target also contains the cross-term $-2\,\mathbb E[(\hat X-\rho)(T_N-\rho)]$, so correlated references can change measured errors and method rankings. These values rely on a static scene, correct registration, equal looks, and independent speckle; they do not establish the 2026 release's representation, registration, temporal stability, or stack membership. They quantify why the per-target stack map is necessary before treating its references as input-independent.

## SAR despeckling

Moran et al.'s [Noisier2Noise](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.11908) trains from one noisy realization by adding an independently sampled synthetic corruption from the same known noise distribution and predicting the singly noisy observation with squared loss. Under the additive model, it recovers the conditional-mean estimate with `2f(z)−z`; its non-additive derivation is a specific multiplicative Bernoulli mask model. The method therefore does not supply a general correction for multiplicative Gamma speckle. The local normalized PNG source-noise law is not established, and its LR/HR pseudo-target share one observation, so this paper does not provide a clean-SAR route for the current pipeline. The official CVPR record is [here](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Moran_Noisier2Noise_Learning_to_Denoise_From_Unpaired_Noisy_Data_CVPR_2020_paper.html); the audit's code search did not locate an author-controlled implementation. See the [known-noise boundary audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/noisier2noise_known_noise_boundary_20261025_v1/report.md).

Lehtinen et al.'s [Noise2Noise](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/lehtinen18a.html) gives Eq. 6 as an indexed clean-target condition, E[ŷ_i | x̂_i] = y_i. For a population with latent S, noisy input X, and noisy target Z, the squared-loss Bayes estimators match when E[Z|X] = E[S|X]; target noise with zero conditional mean given S and independent of X given S is a sufficient construction. This population mean need not equal a sample-specific S. Independent observations of a static signal are a common sufficient construction, not a guarantee for arbitrary dependent degraded pairs. SAR2SAR makes the independent-pair assumption explicit, then adapts it to log-intensity speckle with a distribution-aware likelihood and multitemporal change compensation; it also warns that temporal speckle correlation can remain. Multi-temporal MERLIN analyzes spatial/temporal covariance in complex SLC data and constructs an excluded reference component with preprocessing to meet its independence assumption. These methods establish prior-art assumptions, not that the local normalized PNG pseudo-target satisfies them. See the [source and local-target boundary audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/noisy_pseudo_target_n2n_sar2sar_boundary_20261025_v1/report.md) and the [population-mean clarification](../../experiments/literature_audits/noise2noise_population_mean_clarification_20261025_v1/report.md).

SAR2SAR (Dalsasso, Denis, and Tupin, IEEE JSTARS 2021; DOI `10.1109/JSTARS.2021.3071864`) is a three-stage semi-supervised despeckler: Goodman-Gamma synthetic pretraining, then two real multitemporal SAR stages using change-compensated noisy pairs and a log-intensity co-log-likelihood. The article reports five accurately co-registered Sentinel-1 series for real-data fine-tuning but gives no clean reference for its real-image assessment; it uses homogeneous-region ENL and visual inspection and explicitly warns that ENL favors over-smoothing. This is SAR-only despeckling prior art, not optical guidance or SR/JDSR. The related `deepdespeckling` package exposes single-image inference and bundled weights, but the inspected package does not establish the paper's training lineage or protocol; it is not a matched baseline for local 8-bit SAR/optical PNGs. See the [full-text/code-route audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/sar2sar_primary_fulltext_20261023_v1/report.md).

Speckle2Void (Molini et al., IEEE TGRS 2022; DOI `10.1109/TGRS.2021.3065461`) is a self-supervised Bayesian blind-spot method trained on noisy SAR rather than clean SAR labels or synthetically speckled optical images. Its preprocessing/assumptions around decorrelation and blind spots matter. It does not solve SR or optical fusion.

SAR-DDPM (Perera et al., IEEE GRSL 2023; arXiv:2206.04514v1) is a conditional DDPM for SAR despeckling with cycle-spinning inference. Its author manuscript reports synthetic single-look Gamma speckle (L=1) generated from 15,000 DSIFN remote-sensing images, 30,000 training iterations on an RTX 2080 Ti with ImageNet-pretrained initialization, and T=1,000. On 10 synthetic DSIFN images, the authors report 27.99 dB/0.77 PSNR/SSIM, increasing to 29.42 dB/0.81 with cycle spinning. The real check uses two 256×256 Sentinel-1 images and ENL in four manually selected homogeneous regions; it has no clean SAR reference or paired real-scene fidelity metric. The authors note homogeneous-region distortions for the unspun model and reduced distortions with cycle spinning. Optical imagery supplies synthetic training supervision, not an inference-time guide. This is despeckling prior art, not optical-guided joint SR/despeckling or evidence of calibrated-scatterer fidelity. The public repository's mutable main branch has unresolved static conditioning/configuration mismatches and no verified link to the published runs; see the [full-text/code audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/sar_ddpm_fulltext_code_audit_20261025_v1/report.md).

## SAR super-resolution and joint restoration

### Adjacent camera-pipeline evidence on task order and joint restoration

Qian et al. (ICCP 2022) directly compare learning-based denoising, demosaicing, and SR order in Bayer RAW imaging. Their proposed denoising→SR→demosaicing pipeline improves on the conventional demosaicing→denoising→SR sequence in their camera experiments. They also distinguish sequential models from an end-to-end network with an intermediate joint denoising/SR stage. Xing and Egiazarian (CVPR 2021) earlier compared sequential, partially joint, and fully joint camera restoration while fixing the order as denoising→demosaicing→SR. These are method-level precedents, not SAR evidence: neither studies SAR despeckling/SR, optical guidance, or SAR radiometric safeguards. See the [bounded audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/restoration_order_camera_pipeline_20261024_v1/report.md), the [ICCP 2022 manuscript](https://repository.kaust.edu.sa/bitstreams/0dd1deb2-3b11-47f7-8273-db2211020663/download), and the [CVPR 2021 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Xing_End-to-End_Learning_for_Joint_Image_Demosaicing_Denoising_and_Super-Resolution_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf).

**SARGANet: A GAN-U-net hybrid framework for super-resolution of Sentinel-1 SAR imagery** (Jagrati Talreja and Leila Hashemi Beni, *Earth Science Informatics* 19, article 115, 2026; DOI [10.1007/s12145-026-02164-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12145-026-02164-0)) is adjacent SAR-only ×2 SR work. The open article describes a U-Net/GAN generator with spatial attention and lightweight residual dense blocks. Its abstract describes a dual-encoder using VV and VH together, while the method and ablation sections say separate models are trained for each polarization; the exact cross-polarization implementation is therefore unclear. The paper reports 10 Sentinel-1 scenes from DEEPFLOOD, 728 VH and 725 VV tiles, and an 80/10/10 tile split. It says 10 m Sentinel-1 imagery is reconstructed to a simulated 5 m target and that bicubic downsampling creates LR inputs, but does not clearly identify an independent native 5 m SAR target or fully reconcile the source/target construction. The paper also does not state whether tiles were split by source scene, so the listed 10-scene coverage does not by itself establish a scene-disjoint holdout.

The authors report PSNR/SSIM comparisons against mostly generic-image SR models, a synthetic additive-noise stress test, and ENL/SSI/CV on selected homogeneous floodplain/water regions. The latter metrics have no sufficiently specified ROI/mask in the accessible text, and the limitations section later characterizes evaluation as PSNR/SSIM-based while the conclusion calls ENL future work. Reported PSNR also differs between sections (40.5 dB in the parameter plot discussion; 40.8 dB in the runtime discussion). Most materially, the loss-weight sensitivity study says it used a subset of test data and selected the reported weights from those results; treat that test split as involved in loss-weight selection, not as untouched confirmation. The paper acknowledges that more realistic speckle/geometric degradation and comparisons with SAR-specific methods remain future work. The article links the DEEPFLOOD dataset to [Figshare](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.28328339); no model-code link was identified in the article and dataset terms were not separately checked. SARGANet does not use optical guidance and is not evidence for optical utility, a task-matched joint despeckling/SR protocol, or SAR fallback. Do not use its author-reported scores as a matched baseline for the local ×2 noisy-LR/pseudo-target protocol. [Publisher article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12145-026-02164-0).

**SAR Super-Resolution Using Capella Satellite Imagery** (Sewoong Ahn, Sohee Son, Dongjun Lee, Hyunguk Choi, and Taegyun Jeon; *The International Archives of the Photogrammetry, Remote Sensing and Spatial Information Sciences*, XLVIII-G-2025, 87–92, 2025; DOI [10.5194/isprs-archives-XLVIII-G-2025-87-2025](https://doi.org/10.5194/isprs-archives-XLVIII-G-2025-87-2025)) is SAR-only SR using actual Capella imagery as the HR source at a reported 0.5 m GSD. ### Optical-guided SAR despeckling and predictor reliability

**Optical-Driven Nonlocal SAR Despeckling** (Verdoliva, Gaetano, Ruello, and Poggi, *IEEE Geoscience and Remote Sensing Letters* 12(2), 314–318, 2015; DOI `10.1109/LGRS.2014.2337515`) is direct optical-guided SAR despeckling prior art. The full author-uploaded article specifies a bilateral nonlocal estimator whose predictor weights combine spatial distance, optical-pixel distance, and a SAR log-ratio distance; the optical image selects predictors, while only SAR values are averaged. In parallel, SAR-BM3D supplies a SAR-domain estimate. A soft pixelwise blend combines the two outputs using a sigmoid of a multiscale local ENL map: the optical-guided estimate receives more weight in flat homogeneous areas, and SAR-BM3D receives more weight in heterogeneous/man-made areas where optical and SAR geometry may differ. The reported implementation averages ENL maps for window sizes 7 through 61 in steps of 2, with sigmoid parameters γ=7 and a₀=0.64.

The evaluation uses one real single-look COSMO-SkyMed scene near Caserta (18 July 2011) and a GeoEye-1 guide from 26 July 2010, registered to subpixel precision with SAR as master; parameters were tuned on a separate part of the scene. The article compares Enhanced Lee, SRAD, PPB, and SAR-BM3D, showing three 512×512 clips. Because there is no clean reference and the authors could not jointly simulate corresponding SAR/optical images, they do not run Monte Carlo evaluation. They report ENL on homogeneous boxes and edge-smearing/edge-detectability measures using a multitemporal De Grandi SAR estimate as an approximate reference; the text reports stronger ENL suppression than PPB and the best edge map, while acknowledging an imperfect-classifier weakness near man-made structures. This is meaningful prior art for classifier-controlled local optical contribution and SAR-domain fallback in despeckling. It is not a learned utility estimate, a controlled global/local misregistration test, or joint SAR SR evidence. No code link was confirmed. Sources: [author-uploaded full article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/264937865_Optical-Driven_Nonlocal_SAR_Despeckling), [University of Naples record](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/586479), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/LGRS.2014.2337515).

**Guided patch-wise nonlocal SAR despeckling (GNLM)** (Vitale et al., *IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing* 57(9), 6484–6498, 2019; author manuscript [arXiv:1811.11872](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11872)) replaces the 2015 pixel-wise filter/classifier blend with patch-wise SAR nonlocal averaging. SAR and optical patch distances rank candidate predictors, while a statistical test on SAR-domain patch distance rejects unreliable predictors before averaging; the output is formed only from SAR values. It is a target-free predictor-level rejection mechanism for despeckling, not a learned estimate of expected restoration gain.

The full method models intensity observations as `z=xu`, with unit-mean Gamma-distributed `L`-look speckle `u`. Its normalized patch distance averages a SAR log-ratio distance, and a Neyman–Pearson threshold is calibrated under an equal-signal speckle hypothesis using a Gaussian approximation to the patch-summed distance. The paper's T-SAR sharp setting is `T=1+2σ=1.34` and `S0=256`; its parameter study shows the trade-off directly: no rejection admits artifacts, while an overly strict threshold also rejects useful predictors and leaves residual speckle. This is a classical, predictor-level statistical safeguard rather than a learned image-level fallback.

The paper evaluates on T-SAR and COSMO real-image sets. T-SAR has four 512×512 single-look TerraSAR-X HH clips acquired over Rosenheim on 27 January 2008, paired with manually refined, co-registered Google Earth optical imagery; the nearest guide is from 31 December 2009. COSMO has four 512×512 clips from COSMO-SkyMed (18 July 2011) paired with a co-registered GeoEye-1 image from 26 July 2010. Metrics are ENL and ratio-image structuredness (RIS), supplemented by visual review; the authors caution that RIS and other tested measures correlate poorly with perceived restoration quality because there is no clean reference. For one T-SAR building-change example, the 2009 optical guide shows buildings removed although they remain in the 2008 SAR; the authors report visually similar filtered structures using the stale 2009 guide, the older 2002 guide, or SAR-BM3D. They describe geocoding followed by manual keypoint refinement, but do not report a residual displacement measurement. This is qualitative temporal/content-mismatch evidence, not a controlled spatial-shift sweep or quantitative fallback evaluation. The paper does not address SR or joint despeckling/SR. The official [GNLM code repository](https://github.com/grip-unina/GNLM) is MATLAB-oriented and carries a non-profit-use license. Primary sources: [full arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/1811.11872v1), [IEEE DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TGRS.2019.2906412).

The public author-group [GNLM repository](https://github.com/grip-unina/GNLM) exposes MATLAB entry points including `guidedNLMeans.m` and a demo. Its indexed README lists MATLAB 2017a and summarizes use as informational/nonprofit; the full license and paper-to-commit binding were not retrieved. See the [2026 repository status audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/gnlm_author_repository_status_recheck_20261028_v1/report.md).

**VGSRF-Net** (Yuan et al., *Remote Sensing* 18(1), 111, 2026; DOI `10.3390/rs18010111`, published 28 December 2025) is a close adjacent method because it explicitly reconstructs SAR features from visible-image priors before optical–SAR fusion. ## Diffusion beyond denoising

Ma et al.'s 2026 [DualCDM](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/13/4183) adds full publisher-indexed evidence for dual-domain conditional diffusion in the reverse SAR-to-optical direction. It generates RGB/NIR optical targets from SAR inputs using spatial-frequency complex convolutions, adaptive SAR-feature recalibration, and frequency-domain losses. Its experiments use a randomly sampled spring subset of SEN1-2 and a 15-pair author-built S1S2 dataset; the paper does not establish source-group separation for the patch splits. The new S1S2 SAR pipeline reports Refined Lee despeckling, and the reported outputs/metrics are optical-domain, so this is not optical-guided SAR restoration or joint SAR despeckling/SR. The paper links a public code repository, but its source files, revision-to-table lineage, and data-use terms remain unverified. See the [audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/dualcdm_sar_to_optical_audit_20261024_v1/report.md).

SAR diffusion literature now covers despeckling, single-modality SR, and SAR-only joint SR/despeckling. For despeckling, [SAR-DDPM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04514) uses conditional DDPM with cycle-spinning; [R-DDPM](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.03122) is an arXiv v1 regional conditional-DDPM despeckler that averages overlapping regional noise estimates and uses DDIM. Its manuscript describes Sentinel-1 VV+VH data near Shanghai and synthetic observations formed by adding different levels of Gaussian noise; it gives no sample counts or explicit train/validation/test allocation. Real-image evaluation has no clean reference and uses ENL/EPI. The listed region parameters (`m=64`, `n=16`) conflict with the same section’s statement that `n` is a multiple of `m`. This is SAR-only despeckling, not SR or optical-guided joint restoration, and the synthetic noise statement does not establish a Gamma-speckle observation model. [Paul and Savakis (2025)](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/7/2149) evaluate accelerated sampling and sample aggregation. Their paper reports hallucination concerns and weaker quantitative performance for the unmodified SAR-DDPM variant on its synthetic benchmark, while the proposed modifications improve its results. [ECDM](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17172970) (Guo et al., 2025) is SAR-only conditional DDPM despeckling with predicted variance modulation, wavelet downsampling, and 50-step sampling after 1,000-step training. Its synthetic MS-SAR target is optical imagery, while its real fine-tuning reference is a ten-date SAR average; real-scene evaluation is no-reference. Optical therefore supplies synthetic supervision, not an inference-time guide. Split grouping, whether the input date is included in the temporal average, and the GRD-to-SLC transfer details remain unresolved in the accessible indexed text. For SR, the IEEE-copyrighted [ICSP 2024 proceedings TOC](https://www.proceedings.com/content/078/078593webtoc.pdf) verifies that Zilong Chen, Siqian Zhang, and Boli Xiong authored the paper beginning on page 340. [SharpSAR](https://doi.org/10.7527/S1000-6893.2025.32804) (2026) uses latent diffusion, wavelet-guided high-frequency modulation, and direction-aware structure enhancement under synthetic speckle-plus-blur degradation. ### Posterior sampling, measurement consistency, and consistency models

Its accessible abstract describes approximating a likelihood score from the reverse-transition distribution under a multiplicative-noise model, using a pretrained diffusion prior without paired clean/noisy training. The authors report synthetic and real SAR tests plus robustness checks over speckle intensity, initialization, and algorithm parameters. The publisher full text and code were not retrieved, so this remains abstract-level evidence.

Two neighboring papers formulate diffusion around SAR measurements. [Synthetic Aperture Radar Deep Statistical Imaging Through Diffusion Generative Model Conditional Inference](https://doi.org/10.1109/TGRS.2024.3498442) uses MAP guidance from received SAR signals and reports simulated and RADARSAT imaging experiments. ### SAR-to-EO flow matching: adjacent reverse-direction work

Do, Lee, and Kim's [ReFlowSET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00968) (arXiv preprint posted 2026-09-01) is a conditional latent flow-matching method for **SAR-to-EO translation**. It first compares autoencoder round-trip fidelity for SAR and EO, selects FLUX.2, and trains a 24-block, 509.3M-parameter DiT from scratch. SAR conditioning uses a separate stream for eight blocks before feature fusion; a frozen DINOv3 teacher aligns noisy-EO intermediate features with clean target-EO features during training only. The reported operating point uses Euler integration at 50 NFE. The paper reports QXS-SAROPT as 20,000 pairs with its own 16,001/3,999 train/test split, and SAR2Opt as 1,450/627; its author repository says imagery is not redistributed. ReFlowSET reports best DISTS and tied best FID on QXS-SAROPT, while not maximizing PSNR or SSIM. The paper notes that paired SAR–EO observations can be locally misaligned, but the reviewed full text contains no controlled shift sweep. This is an EO-generation result: optical imagery is the target, not an inference-time guide for producing SAR, so it provides no SAR backscatter fidelity, despeckling, or fallback evidence. Its split does not map the local cache IDs or establish source groups.

Yu, Chiang, Wang, and Zhao's [ContraFM-S2O](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31378) (arXiv preprint posted 2026-09-25) also targets SAR-to-optical translation. It predicts an average velocity rather than an instantaneous velocity for one-step generation and adds contrastive regularization intended to distinguish flows conditioned on different SAR inputs. The authors report QXS and SAR2Opt evaluations with SSIM, FID, LPIPS, PSNR, and latency, and claim 4.4× acceleration over PCGL-GAN; the reported training setup uses eight A100 GPUs. The paper describes the QXS split only as approximately 4:1 train/test and gives no source-scene grouping rule. Its SAR2Opt text gives both 2,076 pairs and a 1,450/627 partition, which sum to 2,077. No code link was identified in the reviewed manuscript. As with ReFlowSET, this reverse-direction task does not establish optical-guided SAR restoration or scatterer-safe generation.

Together these preprints establish active flow-matching work for SAR-conditioned EO generation, not for optical-conditioned SAR restoration. They make flow matching a literature-backed generative family, but do not justify porting their losses, metrics, or data splits to this project. Any future SAR-output flow model would still need a valid SAR target, a matched SAR-only control, and explicit observation/scatterer fidelity checks; it remains deferred behind the data-integrity gate.

### CRDRS: residual diffusion for SAR-to-optical translation

## Implications for this project

1. Optical-guided SR and joint SR/denoising are not new by themselves. OMA-SSR and OS_SR describe inference-time optical guidance for SAR SR; HSFMamba explicitly studies inference-time optics-guided joint SR/denoising; OGSRN/OGRN include training-time optical guidance; DSRKD, ISSMSAR, Amieva et al., Muzeau et al., and Ka-SAR cover SAR-only joint or integrated restoration in different sensors, degradation, and supervision regimes. Ka-SAR uses external optical images in hybrid training-data construction, not same-scene optical guidance at inference.
2. Reported gains across these studies do not establish the same question as the local task. Targets, BM3D use, degradation, matching SAR-only control, splits, metric suites, and optical availability differ. A defensible project question must specify those controls rather than make a general “optical helps SAR” claim.
3. A possible future distinction is an inference-time benefit measured against an exactly task-matched SAR-only joint model with the same fixed noisy LR, pseudo-target, sample list, masks, and full metric set, followed by geometric stress only if aligned utility is supported. Existing literature must be searched further before calling this a gap. The current B1 gate failed, and the B1F pairing diagnostic has mixed metrics; neither promotes a method.
4. The current pairing result establishes model response to pair assignment and a proxy-PSNR difference on one failed-screen checkpoint. It does not establish stable full-metric utility or that optical “contains useful SAR information.”
5. The local 2k representation may have substantial domain gap from calibrated operational SAR. Real-data or source-level evidence is required for broader claims.
6. A diffusion route needs a specific advantage over direct regression, a scientifically meaningful target distribution, and tests for hallucinated scatterers/data consistency. The newer SAR literature supplies both efficiency designs and counterevidence about scattering fidelity; it neither establishes local suitability nor rules it out. Existing diffusion work is not itself a reason to start training.

## Classical/model-based, CNN, transformer, and unfolding references

MuLoG (Deledalle, Denis, Tabti, and Tupin, IEEE Transactions on Image Processing 2017; DOI `10.1109/TIP.2017.2713946`) provides a multi-channel log-domain framework that lets Gaussian denoisers be used within SAR speckle reduction. It is a model-based/statistical bridge, not an optical-guided method. Its complex and multi-channel assumptions should not be confused with this project's single-channel 8-bit representation.

SAR-CNN (Chierchia et al., IGARSS 2017; DOI `10.1109/IGARSS.2017.8128234`) uses residual learning to estimate a speckle component and subtract it from the noisy image; training uses a multitemporal image and multilook approximation. It is an early supervised CNN baseline and illustrates that the target's provenance matters.

Wang et al.'s Swin Transformer plus residual CNN method (IEEE GRSL 2024; DOI `10.1109/LGRS.2023.3345137`) combines local residual convolutions with Swin attention in a U-Net and uses pixel-shuffle downsampling as a post-processing strategy for spatially correlated real speckle. Its abstract reports synthetic and real SAR evaluations. It is a modern despeckling reference but does not address SR or optical guidance.

SAR-CDL (Zhao et al., *Signal Processing* 233, article 109967, 2025; DOI `10.1016/j.sigpro.2025.109967`) proposes an unfolded convolutional dictionary-learning method with a variational multiplicative-noise model and ADMM-based learned parameterization. The [publisher record and indexed article excerpts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165168425000817) describe the model `g = f * n`, with unit-mean Gamma speckle of variance `1/L`. They identify PatternNet as the train/validation/test image source (30,400 256×256 Google Maps-derived images across 38 scene categories), and say 7,600 images are randomly selected and cropped; the visible excerpt does not expose the rest of the split recipe or source-group isolation. The simulated experiments vary equivalent-look number and report PSNR/SSIM. The abstract separately claims real-SAR evaluation, but the accessible record does not identify its scenes/sensors, reference target, or quantitative evaluation protocol. This distinction matters: the described PatternNet corruption supports synthetic despeckling evidence, not training on sensor-native SAR or proof that the synthetic noise reproduces real speckle. That is an inference from the stated image source and degradation, not a judgment about the separate real-data results.

The ScienceDirect article page returned HTTP 403 on direct open. The authors' [2023 SSRN preprint record](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4493650) exposes metadata and an abstract, but its PDF route also returned HTTP 403; the relationship between that version and the 2025 journal article is unresolved. The number of unrolled stages, detailed real-data protocol, code, and journal/preprint changes therefore remain unverified. SAR-CDL is model-unfolding prior art for despeckling, not an optical-guided or SR method and not a matched local baseline.

## Optical-SAR registration literature and scope boundary

Zhang et al., “PLISA: An Optical–SAR Remote Sensing Image Registration Method Based on Pseudo-Label Learning and Interactive Spatial Attention” (*Remote Sensing* 17(21), 3571, 2025; DOI [10.3390/rs17213571](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17213571)), add recent self-supervised registration prior art. The publisher-indexed article text describes an automated pseudo-labeling module (APLM) and a pseudo-twin interaction network (PTIF) with cross-modal interactive attention. The paper explicitly says APLM is applied to already co-registered optical–SAR pairs; this is a keypoint-supervision assumption, not an independent measurement of the native residual alignment. Its reported SEN1-2 registration subset has 9,036 training and 1,260 test pairs, but the reviewed text does not establish source-scene-disjoint allocation or independently surveyed checkpoints. It evaluates matching under translation, scale, rotation/scale, partial-overlap, and noise scenarios; these results do not measure the natural residual distribution of the local cohort or optical utility for SAR restoration. The MDPI HTML/XML routes returned HTTP 429, so details here are limited to official publisher-indexed article sections; no author-controlled code link was found in the reviewed routes. See the [publisher-indexed audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/plisa_optical_sar_registration_publisher_indexed_20261028_v1/report.md).

Hughes and Schmitt's *A Semi-Supervised Approach to SAR-Optical Image Matching* (ISPRS Annals IV-2/W7, 71–78, 2019; DOI [10.5194/isprs-annals-IV-2-W7-71-2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/isprs-annals-IV-2-W7-71-2019)) frames matching as a prerequisite for SAR–optical fusion and uses semi-supervised learning to examine limited paired data and the relationship between supervised and unsupervised matching losses. The official ISPRS page confirms the citation, abstract, and CC BY 4.0 record. Its linked PDF timed out in this review, so the dataset, architecture, metrics, results, and code remain unverified. This is registration prior art, not evidence of restoration-time optical utility or local residual-shift behavior. See the [bibliography crosswalk audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/bibliography_matrix_crosswalk_20261028_v1/report.md).

Bertrand, Bourmaud, Vacar, and Bombrun, “Learning-Based SAR–Optical Registration for Navigation: Insights From a Multiyear, Multiseason Continental-Scale Dataset” (*IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing*, 2025; DOI [10.1109/TGRS.2025.3624474](https://doi.org/10.1109/TGRS.2025.3624474)), introduce MEOW for navigation-oriented SAR–optical registration. The institutional publication record confirms the authors, venue, year, DOI, and HAL record; the HAL text route was blocked in this review, so the dataset, protocol, and result details below come from the accessible author manuscript uploaded by co-author Cornelia Vacar ([manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396834624_Learning-Based_SAR-Optical_Registration_for_Navigation_Insights_From_a_Multiyear_Multiseason_Continental-Scale_Dataset); [institutional record](https://www.ims-bordeaux.fr/researchers-publications/)). This was a targeted review of its dataset, registration method, evaluation protocols, results, and limitations.

MEOW is described as 2,917 European locations covering about 4.9 million km², with 4,096×4,096 tiles at 10 m across four seasons and four years (2018, 2020, 2022, 2023). It contains Sentinel-1 VV/VH and Sentinel-2 RGB, plus DEM, land-cover, and coordinate layers. The paper describes the SAR/optical products as co-registered; its processing section names orthorectification and sub-pixel registration, but does not establish residual-error statistics for the local SEN1-2 cohort. Same-year/season composites use a median filter; optical acquisitions above 40% cloud cover are discarded. The reported representation also clips SAR in dB and gamma-corrects/quantizes optical imagery, so these inputs are not interchangeable with the project's current normalized-PNG domain.

The registration models match a 128×128 SAR patch against a 512×512 optical reference and estimate a 2-D translation. The study compares MCGF, OSMNet, FFT U-Net, and MARU-Net, then evaluates 36 hybrids. It applies Gaussian blur (σ=0.25, 0.5, 1, 2) and synthetic fully-developed speckle with looks L∈{5, 8, 15, 25, 35, 75}; the primary metric is correct matching rate at pixel tolerances. The linked [model-source snapshot](https://github.com/Simon-Bertrand/SAROPTReg-TranslatModels/blob/87932781ebf730c465b43a162e89b696e0f013ae/saroptreg_models/scheme.py#L747-L860) constructs Gamma(shape=L, rate=L), samples it, adds `20 log10(sample)` to the SAR input, clips to [-25, 5], then applies the configured SAR standardization. This verifies the released wrapper's behavior, not that the transformation is physically valid for the source SAR quantity or that this exact code/config produced the paper's tables. The authors report that a pseudo-Siamese OSMNet with CrossCorrCE improves matching by more than 120% in the most degraded cases. This is a registration result, not a SAR reconstruction result. Their manuscript also says performance is sensitive to SAR rotation, and acknowledges simplified degradation assumptions that omit effects such as geometric distortion, layover, and shadow.

The geographic protocol separates eastern and western Europe, but the manuscript's main text and figure caption give opposite train/test directions; do not infer which direction is canonical without resolving that discrepancy. The temporal protocol trains on 2018 pairs and tests other year combinations over the same geography, so it is not spatially disjoint. These protocols are useful registration-generalization prior art, but they do not measure residual alignment in the project's fall-only v2 cohort, test joint SAR despeckling/SR, evaluate SAR scatterer fidelity, or provide SAR-only fallback. The associated [author repository](https://github.com/Simon-Bertrand/SAROPTReg-Insights) links MEOW generation/loading code, registration-model code and weights, and a sample-data submodule; dataset-source terms and paper-table-to-code/checkpoint lineage were not audited. No dataset or local image was downloaded or inspected.

Hänsch et al.'s **SpaceNet 9—Cross-Sensor Alignment of Optical and SAR Imagery** (*IEEE JSTARS* 19, 11491–11502, 2026; DOI `10.1109/JSTARS.2026.3666103`) documents a high-resolution, tie-point-supervised registration benchmark, not a SAR restoration task. The dataset covers three earthquake-affected areas in Türkiye and roughly 800 manually labeled optical–SAR tie points. It pairs 0.31 m WorldView-2 RGB with 0.36–0.45 m, single-channel 8-bit Umbra amplitude imagery in UTM GeoTIFFs; two areas supply public train/public-test sections and a third is withheld for final testing. The score is mean Euclidean displacement error in meters at the labeled tie points. The paper reports nonuniform optical-to-SAR offsets averaging roughly 30–34 m before correction, and the best challenge solutions report 8.5 m on public data and 6.7 m on private data. Strong solutions use pretrained matchers, robust correspondence filtering, patchwise/local estimation, and sometimes global-to-local transforms; rigid global transforms do not perfectly describe all scenes. The article also notes that patch datasets such as SEN1-2 are generally paired by geolocation metadata and often curated to remove strong mismatch, while residual local errors may remain. This supports describing the prospective cohort as source-matched nominal georegistration with fine alignment unverified; it does not measure v2's fall-cohort residuals. The sensors, spatial scale, urban earthquake scenes, and tie-point objective differ from this project's Sentinel-1/2 restoration protocol, and the tie points avoid elevated features affected by SAR layover. Do not transfer SpaceNet 9 registration errors to local data or use it as a restoration benchmark. The [DLR repository](https://elib.dlr.de/225463/) provides the published full text; the [official challenge page](https://spacenet.ai/sn9-challenge/) licenses the SpaceNet dataset CC BY-SA 4.0. The winning-solutions repository separately licenses its code Apache-2.0; original provider-specific terms were not independently reviewed. The [official repository README](https://github.com/SpaceNetChallenge/SpaceNet9) lists only three labeled development image/tie-point triples; public-test tie-point CSVs and the separate-AOI final-test files are hidden, so the accessible labels do not provide held-out validation. No data were downloaded. See the [rights and split audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/spacenet9_rights_split_screen_20261025_v1/report.md).

The 2023 SOPatch study by Xu et al. is also relevant to registration provenance. Its full author-uploaded article says the original SEN1-2 pairs have low registration accuracy because they rely on geographic information alone. The authors reconstruct larger SAR and optical scenes from the overlapping 256×256 SEN1-2 patches, use RIFT matches refined with CFOG, estimate an affine registration, and then cut non-overlapping 512×512 pairs. Their SEN1-2 contribution uses only spring and winter scenes (2,709 and 2,719 patches respectively); it does not cover the fall-only validation folders in the prospective cohort v2. The article gives no residual-error distribution for those fall pairs, and the few-to-dozens-of-pixels figure in the preceding discussion applies to WHU-SEN-City, so it must not be transferred to SEN1-2. Exact pixel authentication of cohort v2 establishes source identity, not fine co-registration. The publisher page returned HTTP 403; the author-uploaded [full article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372937571_SAR-optical_feature_matching_A_large-scale_patch_dataset_and_a_deep_local_descriptor) and [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2023.103433) were reviewed. The article provides a Baidu link but also says data are available on request; no data archive, license, or mapping from the fall cohort to a fine-registered derivative was verified.

GDWF (Luo et al., Remote Sensing 2025; DOI `10.3390/rs17152544`) combines gradient and local-standard-deviation descriptors with confidence-weighted feature matching for heterogeneous optical-SAR scenes. It is a registration method, not a restoration network. Its confidence weights decide how to match descriptors during registration; that does not establish a calibrated downstream per-pixel decision about whether optical content should influence SAR reconstruction.

OSR-Net (Yaqi Zhang, Shengbo Chen, Xitong Xu, Jiaqi Yang, Yuqiao Suo, Jinchen Zhu, Menghan Wu, Aonan Zhang, and Qiqi Li; *Remote Sensing* 2026, 18(2), 333; DOI `10.3390/rs18020333`) is an end-to-end optical-SAR registration framework. Publisher-indexed article text describes optical, original-SAR, and affine-offset-SAR inputs; modality-specific ResNet-18 features; multi-scale channel attention; affine-transform prediction; and a spatial transformer. The publisher highlight reports RMSE below 1.25 px on MultiResSAR, an author-reported registration result. The article identifies a low-resolution MultiResSAR subset, but its exact size, split, and reference-point independence remain unresolved. Its data statement links arXiv:2502.01002, which is a distinct MultiResSAR review by Wenfei Zhang et al., not an OSR-Net manuscript; that link does not establish OSR-Net code or paper-to-run lineage. Registration accuracy is not restoration quality or a residual-mismatch threshold; the method does not establish optical benefit for SAR despeckling/SR or a SAR-only fallback. See the [publisher-indexed source audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/osr_net_publisher_indexed_text_update_20261028_v1/report.md).

Yu and Jung's **Attention OffsetNet: Translation-Free Transfer Learning for Optical–SAR Registration With Full-Scene Subpixel Refinement** (IEEE JSTARS, accepted/in press 2026; DOI `10.1109/JSTARS.2026.3731425`) is geometric-registration prior art. The [University of Seoul institutional record](https://pure.uos.ac.kr/en/publications/attention-offsetnet-translation-free-transfer-learning-for-optica/) exposes metadata and the author abstract, but the full article and code were not available in this screen. The abstract describes optical–optical pretraining followed by optical–SAR offset-regression fine-tuning, naming KOMPSAT-3A and TerraSAR-X. It reports patch-level RMSE of 8.87 px X / 7.99 px Y, then separately reports about 0.8 px X / 0.2 px Y full-scene residual after patch offsets are filtered and aggregated with RANSAC. The latter is an author-reported scene-level result, not patch-level network accuracy. Split membership, offset-label construction, per-scene residuals, and paper-to-code lineage remain open. The study does not evaluate SAR reconstruction, optical benefit over SAR-only restoration, or fallback under residual mismatch; its metrics cannot be transferred to the local fall-pair cohort. See the [bounded audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/attention_offsetnet_author_page_screen_20261024_v1/report.md).

SOMA (Wang et al., AAAI 2026, 40(12), 9757–9765; DOI `10.1609/aaai.v40i12.37939`) is a dense SAR–optical registration method, not a restoration model. The full author manuscript is available on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13168), and the authors link a public [MIT-licensed code repository](https://github.com/traslauc/SOMA). FGE enhances multiscale, multidirectional feature gradients; GLAM refines affine and flow fields from coarse to fine, with frozen DINOv2 features at the coarsest level. A pixelwise certainty map is used for training supervision and omitted at inference. The paper uses SOPatch's finely registered SEN1-2 pairs because the original pairs are not strictly aligned, evaluates on GFGE_SO, and reports cross-dataset results on WHU-SEN-City and OSdataset. The experiment section states a main synthetic transform setting of translation up to 32 px, scale variation 0.2, and rotation within ±5°; it says the ablation extends translation to 50 px and rotation to ±20°. Training is reported as 100 epochs, batch size 4, AdamW at `5e-5`, five warmup epochs, and two RTX 4090 GPUs. The paper reports SEN1-2 CMR@1px of 86.67% in its main comparison (+12.29 percentage points over SO-ConvNeXt) and GFGE_SO CMR@1px of 79.38% (+18.50 points over RMSO-ConvNeXt). Its full ablation table reports 87.53% for SOMA. The publicly visible [`main` loader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/traslauc/SOMA/main/datasets/dataloader.py) instead hardcodes translations sampled from ±50 px, rotations from ±20°, and scale fixed at 1.0; there are no exposed transform-range arguments. The [training script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/traslauc/SOMA/main/train.py) enables this transform for both train and validation, and the [test script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/traslauc/SOMA/main/test.py) enables it for test. Neither visible script fixes the random seed, so validation and test transforms are stochastic. The reachable public history has six commits on `main`; commit [`1003432`](https://github.com/traslauc/SOMA/commit/1003432fa921e2dde2fe43fadf1f706a4c4172c6) first adds the loader and train/test scripts with these wider transforms, and the later commits change only the README. No earlier matching loader/config exists in the reachable history. The tracked tree has no run-specific transform config or checkpoint-to-table mapping. As versioned, the public code defaults to the stated ablation-like range and contains no configuration for the paper's stated main range; the exact source/run provenance for the published comparison and ablation tables remains unresolved. Do not treat the current public loader as a reproducible implementation of the main comparison. The code and paper concern registration accuracy; neither demonstrates downstream restoration quality under residual error or a SAR-safe optical rejection/fallback mechanism. [AAAI record](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37939), [arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13168), [official code](https://github.com/traslauc/SOMA), [first code-bearing commit](https://github.com/traslauc/SOMA/commit/1003432fa921e2dde2fe43fadf1f706a4c4172c6).

The publisher article preview reports 593,335 pairs from 319 cities across 78 countries at 0.16–0.98 m resolution; it reports 2.79 px RMSE on common scenes and 3.08 px on unseen disaster scenes. These are registration results and do not establish restored-SAR fidelity or optical fallback under residual mismatch. The authors' [repository README](https://github.com/vision-heng/GUSO) says dataset links are exposed after a request-form submission, lists separate train/validation/test archives for five general scene categories and a disaster test archive, and states academic use only, no commercial use, and prior written consent for secondary development or modification. The repository's split names do not establish source-group independence, and the underlying provider terms and archive contents were not audited. The publisher full article was not retrievable in this review, so detailed protocol and data lineage remain open. GUSO is an ultra-high-resolution registration resource, not a restoration benchmark or a substitute for the local 10 m SAR restoration protocol.

Chen et al.'s **RQ-Net** (*Information Fusion* 123, article 103278, 2025; DOI `10.1016/j.inffus.2025.103278`) is a describe-then-detect optical–SAR registration network with separate local-descriptor and multi-scale registration-aware cross-modal feature paths. The publisher-indexed abstract says it was evaluated on three paired optical–SAR datasets; the accessible excerpts do not establish dataset identities/splits, injected shift/warp coverage, or residual-error distributions. ### Registration-error sensitivity, fusion, and confidence cues

Lian et al., “Structure-Confidence Guided Phase Congruency and Cascade Matching for Registration of Optical and SAR Images” (*Remote Sensing* 18(14), 2322, 2026; DOI [10.3390/rs18142322](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18142322)), propose RNW-PC, which weights phase congruency with relative total variation and a nonsubsampled contourlet transform, and WSC coarse-to-fine correspondence matching with distance and directional constraints. The publisher-indexed abstract reports evaluation on 60 image pairs from four data groups and claims improved correct-match count and registration accuracy against point-based methods. Direct HTML/PDF/XML routes were unavailable in the current reader, so dataset-specific counts, grouping, independent tie-point references, controlled translation/warp severity, and code/run lineage remain open. This is structure-confidence prior art for registration, not evidence of SAR-output restoration, optical utility, or a safe fallback under residual mismatch. See D-2026-10-28-030 and the [bounded audit](../../experiments/literature_audits/registration_structure_confidence_phase_congruency_20261028_v1/report.md).

Wu et al. (2022; DOI `10.1016/j.jag.2022.102868`) use simulated geometric registration errors to compare pixel-, feature-, and decision-level SAR–optical fusion for land-cover classification. The publisher abstract reports that feature-level fusion was least sensitive, optical-referenced fusion was more tolerant than SAR-referenced fusion, heterogeneous regions were more affected, and fusion did not always beat optical-only classification. This establishes downstream sensitivity in a classification setting; it does not measure restoration-image fidelity. MHFNet (Zhang et al., *ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing*, 2026; DOI `10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2025.10.027`) addresses misaligned SAR–optical ship detection with deformable feature alignment, content-guided attention, an uncertainty-aware shape-IoU loss, and distance-based probabilistic fusion of detections. The official publisher's indexed methods text clarifies that US-Loss downweights detector training examples affected by cross-modal box-label shifts; this is example/annotation-level detector supervision, not a pixelwise optical-use estimate for restored SAR. Its output is detections and boxes, not reconstructed SAR intensity. The accessible publisher text names QXS and Suez but does not establish exact dataset versions/splits or a controlled offset sweep; direct page open returned HTTP 403. The publisher record links the authors' [repository](https://github.com/wsdmw1/MHFNet). ### Misalignment-aware multimodal fusion outside SAR restoration

Wang et al.'s **IMF** (*IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems for Video Technology* 34(11), 10944–10958, 2024; DOI `10.1109/TCSVT.2024.3412743`) jointly trains a one-stage coarse-to-fine dense registration network and an infrared-visible fusion network. A frozen visible-to-pseudo-infrared translator first reduces the modality gap; deformation-field fusion and progressive feature refinement then estimate and apply a dense warp before the Transformer-Conv fusion stage. The paper synthesizes affine-plus-elastic misalignment from aligned IR/VIS and NIR/VIS data, trains on its slight setting, evaluates robustness at moderate/severe synthetic settings, and selects 477 visibly misaligned FLIR pairs as real-world examples. It reports registration MSE/MI/NCC and fused-image CC/MI/VIF/SSIM/Qabf. This is useful precedent for progressive registration tied to a downstream fusion loss, but the output is an IR/VIS fused image; it does not test SAR restoration fidelity, SAR scatterers, or an SAR-only fallback. The primary [IEEE record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10553339/) and [arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11165) were reviewed. Xiao et al.'s **HMMF** (*Proceedings of AAAI* 40(13), 11005–11013, 2026; DOI `10.1609/aaai.v40i13.38078`) also jointly optimizes registration and IR/VIS fusion. The paper trains on synthetically affine/elastic-misaligned IR/VIS data from RoadScene, M3FD, and MSIFT; it reports registration NCC/MI and fused-image QNCIE/MI/VIF/Qabf, with increased deformation used for a robustness evaluation. Its code is linked by the [AAAI paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38078) at the [author repository](https://github.com/xiao-eee/HMMF). ### Recent multi-source SAR–optical alignment datasets

The version-of-record article **“3MOS: a multi-source, multi-resolution, and multi-scene optical-SAR dataset with insights for multi-modal image matching”** (Yibin Ye et al., *Visual Intelligence* 3(1), 1–27, article 19, 2025; DOI `10.1007/s44267-025-00091-0`) describes 113,000 optical–SAR pairs from 11 regions and five SAR satellites, at 3.5–12.5 m, grouped into eight terrain/land-cover scene categories. ## SEN12TP as a conditional source-provenance candidate

Roßberg and Schmitt introduce **SEN12TP** for Sentinel-1 SAR-to-NDVI regression, not restoration. Pair gaps are at most 12 hours and typically about six hours; the paper notes that the different orbital times can expose vegetation to diurnal water changes, so “timely paired” does not mean simultaneous. Sentinel-1 GRD is reported as sigma-nought dB after calibration, terrain correction, and orthorectification; Sentinel-2 is L2A BOA reflectance. The rasters use 10 m pixel spacing, while the paper states the Sentinel-1 effective spatial resolution is about 20 × 22 m. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41064-023-00238-y).

## 2026-10-30 补充：SAR 联合恢复与通用可靠性/效率先例

- **SAR 联合去斑与超分：** DSRKD（TGRS，2024）以知识蒸馏联合去斑与超分；ISSMSAR（Remote Sensing，2024）研究多时相 SAR 集成恢复；Amieva 等（Acta Astronautica，2026）利用 Sentinel-1 IW/SM 模式进行非生成式联合恢复。
- **光学引导：** HSFMamba（JSTARS，2025）直接研究光学引导 SAR 联合超分和去噪；OGSRN（2022）将光学空间反馈用于训练；OGSFlow README 描述光学引导 UAV-SAR 两阶段流 SR，证据级别仍是仓库说明。
- **可靠性自适应：** Ada-RefSR（ICLR，2026）以隐式相关性门控控制自然图像参考对单步扩散超分的贡献，不能直接等同于 SAR—光学 JDSR。
- **少步方法：** NeurIPS 2025 的 Blockwise Flow Matching、LLE 和 MeanFlow 分别涉及分块流生成、扩散逆问题外推和单步图像生成；这些都未验证 SAR 恢复质量。
- **未核实线索：** 区域专用扩散 SAR JDSR DOI 记录缺少本轮可核实的出版方正文，继续标为开放核验任务。详见[前沿文献对照报告](../../experiments/literature_audits/optical_sar_generative_frontier_20261030_v1/report.md)。

## 2026-11-05 补充：算子感知流与条件后验覆盖

Shoushtari 等人的 [*Fast and Faithful: Principled Conditional Flow Matching for Inverse Problems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12953) 研究带已知线性观测算子和加性 Gaussian 噪声的图像逆问题，以算子相关的条件更新训练 flow，并在自然图像逆问题上评测。Zeng 等人的 [*Twist Flow for Inverse Problems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09281) 通过增广传输处理条件生成欠离散问题，报告自然图像合成退化上的观测一致性与样本扩散分析。

两项工作缩小了“算子条件流”与“检查条件生成样本覆盖”的通用方法新颖性范围，但不验证 SAR 去斑、SAR 联合超分、光学引导或本地可靠性回退。其线性 Gaussian 观测假设不能直接移植到当前归一化 8-bit PNG 与 noisy pseudo-target；本项目若检验观测条件，仍须先核验数值表示和退化模型。相关条目及一手来源边界见[文献审查报告](../../experiments/literature_audits/flow_operator_posterior_support_20261105_v1/report.md)和[相关工作矩阵](related_work_matrix.csv)。
